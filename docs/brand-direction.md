# Top 100 brand direction

Chosen direction: **green / navy / white** concept.

This replaces the personal-Micro.blog feel with a distinct Top 100 identity that can extend across the editorial site, Stats & History, Tournaments, Awards, the Publishing Desk, and the separate Regen site.

## Core identity

- Primary wordmark: **Top 100**
- Primary palette:
  - Deep navy: `#0B1F3B`
  - Emerald: `#10B981`
  - Slate: `#334155`
  - Light grey: `#CBD5E1`
  - Off-white: `#F8FAFC`
- Typography direction: clean modern sans-serif, with **Inter** as the working reference.
- Visual language: modern, editorial, data-driven, with restrained football/pitch geometry rather than club-style heraldry.
- Tone: community, competition, history, progression.

## Brand family

Use the same visual system across:

- **Top 100** — main editorial/community site
- **Top 100 Stats & History**
- **Top 100 Tournaments**
- **Top 100 Awards**
- **Top 100 Regen** — separate sister game world, same family
- manager-facing tools such as the Publishing Desk and shared voting system

Sub-brand icons can use simple functional symbols (stats bars, trophy, star, regen/next-generation mark) while keeping typography and colour consistent.

## Main-site implementation

The Micro.blog site should stop presenting David Marsden's avatar/name as the primary masthead identity.

Target masthead:

- Top 100 wordmark/logo
- ecosystem navigation: `Top 100 · Stats & History · Tournaments · Awards · Regen`
- utility navigation: `About · Rules · Support · Contact · Posts Archive · Write`
- dark navy masthead/background with emerald active/accent states

David remains visible at post level via bylines, not as the site brand.

## Homepage direction

1. Strong Top 100 masthead/hero
2. Current activity / latest editorial posts
3. Explore the ecosystem: Stats & History, Tournaments, Awards, Regen
4. Community/governance links: Rules, Support, Contact, Write
5. Posts Archive / historical blog access

## Design-system principles

- consistent buttons, pills, cards, tables, forms and footer language across apps
- emerald for primary action/active state; navy for structure; off-white/light grey for readability
- strong mobile behaviour and accessible contrast
- avoid decorative football clichés unless they serve hierarchy or recognition
- retain personality through copy and editorial imagery rather than inconsistent UI styling

## Next implementation step

Apply this visual direction first to the Micro.blog theme, then review each surviving app/site against the same design system.
