## Historical context

The site originated on Blogger and was later imported to WordPress. The audit therefore needs to distinguish at least three layers:

1. content that is intrinsically valuable Top 100 history;
2. metadata/structure created to organise the site at different points in its life; and
3. migration/platform artefacts that have little or no editorial value.

## Audit questions

### Posts

- What date range does the archive cover?
- Which posts are genuine editorial/history pieces versus routine notices/results that now have better structured homes?
- Which old posts remain highly linked or important as historical references?
- Are there duplicate/imported versions of posts?
- What is the scale and nature of the uncategorised material?
- Which posts depend on dead embeds, old Blogger markup or obsolete WordPress blocks?

### Pages

Every page should eventually receive one disposition:

- **keep** — still useful as a standalone page;
- **merge** — combine with another page or destination;
- **move** — better owned by Archive/Tournaments/Awards/Rules;
- **redirect** — legacy URL should resolve elsewhere;
- **retire** — no continuing value beyond the preserved export.

### Categories and tags

The current taxonomy should be treated as evidence of past editorial practice, not as a specification for the new site.

Audit for:

- near-duplicates and spelling variants;
- season labels that might be better represented elsewhere;
- club/manager categories that duplicate Archive functionality;
- competition labels that belong in Tournaments;
- categories created for one-off workflows;
- tags imported from Blogger or generated by WordPress/plugins;
- high-value editorial categories worth preserving.

### URLs

Build an inventory of:

- current permalink patterns;
- important pages;
- high-value historical posts;
- category/tag archive URLs;
- media URLs;
- URLs linked from the specialist apps or elsewhere in the Top 100 ecosystem.

URL preservation matters especially for the eventual move to Micro.blog.

### Media

The WXR export is not a substitute for a media backup. WordPress.com's separate media export/download should be retained alongside the content export.

The media audit should identify:

- broken/missing attachment references;
- Blogger-era externally hosted images;
- duplicate media;
- important logos, competition graphics and historical images that should be preserved as canonical assets.

## Deliverables

The detailed audit should eventually produce machine-readable inventories under `audit/`, including:

- `posts.csv`
- `pages.csv`
- `categories.csv`
- `tags.csv`
- `post-types.csv`
- `urls.csv`
- `redirects.csv`

These generated files are intended to support decisions, not replace the source backup.
