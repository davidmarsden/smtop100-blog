# Playing it differently: the mavericks of Season 28

Most Top 100 managers set their teams up in much the same way. So who did something different this season, and did it work? The AI has crunched the numbers from **1,348 league match reports** from Season 28, about seven in ten of the season's league games. Each report shows both teams' starting tactics and starting XIs.

## What decides a match: ratings and home advantage

Before the mavericks, the two things every manager has to play with: the quality of their XI, and where the game is played.

**Ratings matter most.** The gap in average rating between the two starting XIs is the best single predictor of a result:

| Home XI compared with away XI | Home win | Draw | Away win | Home points per game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4+ points weaker | 5% | 10% | 85% | 0.26 |
| 3 points weaker | 18% | 15% | 68% | 0.68 |
| 2 points weaker | 29% | 27% | 44% | 1.14 |
| 1 point weaker | 38% | 22% | 40% | 1.36 |
| Level | 41% | 21% | 38% | 1.44 |
| 1 point stronger | 49% | 20% | 32% | 1.66 |
| 2 points stronger | 52% | 24% | 25% | 1.79 |
| 3 points stronger | 57% | 13% | 30% | 1.85 |
| 4+ points stronger | 86% | 12% | 2% | 2.69 |

Every point of average rating is worth between a tenth and a quarter of a point a game. Once the gap reaches four points, the stronger side almost always wins.

**Home advantage is real, but smaller.** Home sides took **1.48** points a game (42% wins) against **1.31** away (37%). With level XIs, home sides won 41% and away sides 38%. Playing at home is worth about **one point of average rating**, roughly three extra points over a season's home games.

## A fair test

Because ratings matter so much, judging a manager by points alone isn't fair. So for every team in every match, the AI worked out the points a team with that rating gap, home or away, would expect. Then it measured how far each club finished **above or below that**, in points per game. +0.10 is about four points over a season.

## The mavericks who made it work

| Club | Finished | Above expected | What was different |
|---|---|---|---|
| SL Benfica | D2 champions | **+0.66** | A defensive mentality |
| Villarreal CF | D5 6th | +0.39 | 4-4-2 |
| Girondins Bordeaux | D5 9th | +0.38 | Five different formations, with weaker XIs than their opponents |
| SSC Napoli | D4 champions | +0.35 | Neither attacking nor defensive, and direct passing |
| RCD Espanyol | D1 12th | +0.34 | Defensive and slow, with weaker XIs than their opponents |
| Udinese Calcio | D2 runners-up | +0.31 | Defensive, sitting deep |
| SS Lazio | D5 4th | +0.27 | 4-4-2 |
| Manchester City | D1 17th, relegated | +0.26 | Defensive, with XIs two rating points weaker than their opponents |

**Benfica** won Division 2 with a defensive set-up, doing two-thirds of a point a game better than their line-ups predicted. Only three clubs did better (more on them below). **Udinese** went up behind them, also defensive and sitting deep.

**Espanyol** stayed up in Division 1, usually fielding the weaker XI. Glen Mullan's young side took a third of a point a game more than expected: "We stayed up against the odds."

**Manchester City** were relegated, but their XIs were two rating points weaker than the opposition's on average. They still beat what that predicted by a quarter of a point a game.

**Bordeaux** are the exception to the rule below: five formations, and still well above expected.

The three clubs who beat their line-ups by the most played it straight. **AZ Alkmaar** (+0.92), **Atalanta** (+0.84) and **PSV** (+0.81) were Division 5's three automatically promoted clubs. **FC Porto** (+0.72) won Division 1.

## The mavericks who didn't

| Club | Finished | Above expected | What was different |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feyenoord | D4 20th | **−1.04** | 4-2-3-1 A, neither attacking nor defensive |
| Juventus | D1 20th | **−0.90** | Neither attacking nor defensive |
| FC Schalke 04 | D5 13th | −0.55 | Very defensive, pressing in their own area, long balls |
| ACF Fiorentina | D5 14th | −0.52 | 4-1-3-2, defensive and deep, with stronger XIs |
| Internazionale | D2 15th | −0.51 | Defensive and deep, with XIs over a point stronger |
| Fenerbahçe SK | D5 19th | −0.46 | 4-3-3 |
| Club Brugge KV | D5 15th | −0.28 | 3-4-3, with XIs 1.5 points stronger |
| Borussia Dortmund | D1 6th | −0.14 | 4-3-3, with XIs nearly two points stronger |

**Feyenoord** and **Juventus** were the two biggest underperformers in Top 100, a full point a game below what their line-ups predicted.

**Inter** and **Fiorentina** show that going defensive can backfire. Both usually fielded the stronger XI, sat deep and finished well below expectations.

**Brugge's 3-4-3** produced the most entertaining games in Top 100, at 4.5 goals a game, but turned XIs 1.5 points stronger than their opponents' into a 15th-place finish.

**Dortmund** finished a respectable 6th in Dario Saviano's final season. With XIs nearly two points stronger than their opponents', though, the line-ups predicted more.

## Trying too hard to beat the system

Hamburger SV are a cautionary tale, and I can say that because they're my club.

I changed formation and tactics from game to game, depending on the opponent's strength and whether we were home or away: attacking at home, a back five or a back three away at stronger sides. Over the season, Hamburger used **eight different set-ups**, and the most common one in only a quarter of games. We took **29 points from the 34 games** recorded, against **47** that our line-ups predicted.

It wasn't just me. Across Top 100:

- The **47 clubs that stuck with one set-up** in at least nine games out of ten finished **+0.15** a game above expected.
- The **53 clubs that switched more often** finished **−0.14** below it.
- When those switchers did change their set-up, they did worse than expected **in every situation**: against stronger XIs (−0.19), against level XIs (−0.33) and against weaker ones (−0.32). In their usual set-up they were about where their line-ups predicted.

Struggling teams may switch *because* they're struggling, so this doesn't prove switching costs points. But the managers who picked a plan and stuck to it did best, and the ones trying to out-think every opponent, me included, did worst.

## Health warning

This shows what went with good results, not what caused them. These are starting tactics, and many managers change things during a match. The records cover about 70% of league games: most of Division 1, and Divisions 2 to 5 up to late September or early October.
