# Stats & History: more than an archive

Top 100 has been running for a long time.

That means we have two different kinds of history.

There is the history of what we **wrote** — old match reports, predictions, reviews, announcements, team pieces and arguments preserved in the blog archive.

And then there is the history of what actually **happened** — who managed where, who won what, how clubs moved between divisions, and the records built up over season after season.

Those are not the same thing, which is why Stats & History now has a home of its own:

[https://archive.smtop100.blog/](https://archive.smtop100.blog/)

## What Stats & History is for

The site is the structured memory of Top 100.

Instead of asking you to search through old posts and hope somebody happened to mention the fact you want, it brings historical data together in a form that can be explored directly.

That includes the long-term record of clubs, managers, seasons, promotions, relegations and other competition history.

It is the place to answer questions such as:

- Who managed this club three seasons ago?
- Where did this team finish in Season 21?
- Which managers have the strongest long-term records?
- How did a club get from Division 4 to Division 1?
- What happened across a manager's Top 100 career?

Those are data questions, not blog-search questions.

## Why call it Stats & History?

Because **Archive** on its own had become ambiguous.

The main blog also has a chronological archive. That archive is useful if you want to browse old Top 100 writing by date.

Stats & History is different. It is built around the competition itself.

So the distinction is now deliberately clearer:

**Posts Archive** = what Top 100 published.

**Stats & History** = what happened in Top 100.

Both matter. They just serve different jobs.

## The history survived the move

One of the priorities during the move away from WordPress was not to treat old material as disposable.

Top 100 is not a brand-new website pretending it began in 2026. The point of rebuilding the ecosystem was to make more than a decade of history easier to use, not to wipe the slate clean.

The main blog keeps the articles and contributor bylines. Stats & History keeps the structured competitive record.

Together, they tell a much richer story than either could on its own.

## Where this can go next

The really interesting part is what becomes possible now that manager identity, tournament data and historical records are gradually becoming more consistent.

One of the obvious future ideas is a proper **My Top 100** career view: clubs managed, seasons, honours, promotions, tournament runs and Awards attached to one verified manager identity.

We are not pretending all of that exists today. But the rebuilt Stats & History site is one of the foundations that makes it possible.

For now, if you are looking for an old article, use Search or the Posts Archive on the main site.
