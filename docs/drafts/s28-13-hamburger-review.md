# Too Clever by Half

# How Hamburg Talked Themselves Out of Division 1

_Eighteenth place, 32 points and eight different formations. In Season 28 Hamburg had the squad to stay up, and a manager determined to outwit the match engine. The match engine won._

* * *

Some relegations are a slow death. Ours was more of a self-inflicted wound.

Two seasons after [surviving by the skin of our teeth](https://smtop100.blog/2026/01/22/staying-alive-hamburg-defy-the/) and one after [entertaining our way to 12th](https://smtop100.blog/2026/06/19/the-entertainers/), Hamburg went into Season 28 with Kevin De Bruyne, the strongest spine the club has had in years, and genuine hopes of a top-half finish.

We went down with a game to spare.

* * *

# The Plan

The summer plan looked sensible on paper. De Bruyne would pull the strings in a 4-3-3 alongside Casemiro and Kees Smit. Against the stronger sides we'd return to the 5-2-3 low block that had served us so well at the end of S27.

In practice the 4-3-3 started only five league games. I soon went back to the familiar 4-2-3-1 B, which started 24 of our 38.

Our first-team XI averaged 90.4. That's not a relegation squad.

After six games we were 12th, which felt about right.

After ten we were 19th, which didn't.

We spent most of the rest of the season in 19th, and only climbed to 18th with two games left, by which point the only question was who would be joining us.

* * *

Our defence has been absolutely woeful for all three of our seasons in Division 1. We conceded 85 in S26, 77 in S27 and 73 this season: a little better each time, and still the second-worst in the division.

The difference was at the other end. In the two previous seasons our forwards scored enough to keep us up, and last time they even got us into the SMFA Shield. This season they didn't fire at all, while we carried on shipping goals at an alarming rate. Last season we scored 72. This season we managed 44, and only Juventus, Manchester City and CSKA were less potent.

The Entertainers had become the Spectators.

Our matches still produced 3.08 goals a game, mid-table for chaos, but this time most of them were going in at our end. And the Volksparkstadion, last season's fortress, became a place where visiting teams quite enjoyed themselves: 6 wins from 19 at home, and 2 from 19 away.

* * *

# Trying to Outwit the Match Engine

![Hamburger SV Season 28 points per game by starting formation](https://smtop100.blog/uploads/2026/hsv-s28-formations.png)

I wrote about this in the [tactics review](https://smtop100.blog/2026/11/18/playing-it-differently-the-mavericks/), so I'll be brief, and honest.

I changed formation and tactics game by game, depending on the opponent and whether we were home or away. Attacking at home. A back five or a back three away at stronger sides. By the end of the season Hamburg had used **eight different set-ups**, and the most common one in only a quarter of games.

In the 34 games recorded, our line-ups were good enough for **47 points**. We took **29**.

Our usual 4-2-3-1 B took 23 points from its 24 starts. That's 0.96 a game, survival pace and no more. The clever stuff was worse. The 3-2-2-2-1 brought 3 points from 5 games. The 5-4-1 brought 1 from 4.

Across the whole of Top 100, the clubs that picked a plan and stuck to it finished above what their line-ups predicted. The tinkerers finished below it.

I was the tinkerer-in-chief.

As I put it on the newsfeed the night we went down: "trying to outwit the match engine [is] always a stupid idea."

Lesson learned. Probably.

* * *

# Fine Margins, Wrong Side

Every one of our eight league wins was by a single goal: 2-1, 3-2, 1-0. We won at Barcelona. We beat Manchester City 3-2.

The defeats were rather less tidy: 0-4 at Hertha, 1-4 at Leicester, 0-3 at home to Leicester and 3-5 at home to Aston Villa.

A seven-game winless run in September did the damage. From late October there was nothing left: one point from the last four games, and relegation was confirmed after a 2-1 defeat at Aston Villa with a game still to play.

We finished four points behind Manchester City in 17th.

* * *

# Cup Comfort

Away from the league, Hamburg looked like a different team.

In the **SMFA Shield**, our reward for last season's goals, we topped Group H unbeaten: five wins and a draw, 20 scored and 4 conceded against Cremonese, FC København and Slavia Praha. Then we drew Manchester City in Round 1 and lost both legs 2-1.

The **Top 100 Cup** was the run of the season. We beat Shakhtar Donetsk 2-1, Villarreal 3-2 and champions-to-be FC Porto 2-0 at the Volksparkstadion. We got past Swansea on penalties after a 3-3 draw, and won 2-1 at AC Milan in the quarter-final.

The semi-final at Hellas Verona was a step too far. We lost 3-0 to the eventual winners.

The **Top 100 Shield** was brief: 2-0 at West Brom in Round 2.

A Cup semi-final and an unbeaten Shield group don't make up for relegation. But they do suggest the squad was better than the league table, and the manager rather worse.

# Player of the Season: Hugo Ekitiké

* * *

![Hugo Ekitiké, Hamburger SV Player of Season 28: 7.59 average, 8 of 11 Man of the Match awards](https://smtop100.blog/uploads/2026/hsv-s28-ekitike.png)

* * *

In a season with very little to celebrate, Hugo Ekitiké was the exception.

It didn't start that way. He scored twice in the first three weeks, then went two and a half months without a league goal, from mid-July to early October. Then, just as the season was slipping away, he found his touch: four goals in five games in October.

His 6 goals and 5 assists don't jump off the page in a team that scored 44, but his 7.59 average was the best in the squad. He won **8 of the team's 11 Man of the Match awards**.

When Hamburg played well, Ekitiké usually had something to do with it. When we didn't, he was usually the only one trying.

The same couldn't be said of Mason Greenwood, our Player of the Season last time. He laid on 8 assists but didn't score a single league goal all season. His only goals came in the cups: two in the SMFA Shield group in early July, and one at Swansea in the Top 100 Cup. Last season he had 11 league goals and 21 assists. That drop alone goes a long way to explaining where our 28 missing goals went.

Honourable mentions go to Leroy Sané (9 assists, 7.50 average) before he left in the Emiliano Martínez deal. They also go to Kevin De Bruyne (8 goals, 7.39), who gave us one excellent season and then a very good transfer.

Casemiro matched De Bruyne's 8 goals. Not quite last season's 12, but still a remarkable return from a 34-year-old whose job title begins with "defensive".

# Back in the Red

Last season I proudly reported a transfer profit of almost £50m.

This season we spent £62.3m and received £28.0m, a loss of £34.4m.

Most of that went in the summer: Fellipe Jack, Andreas Borgersen and Lassine Ouédraogo cost us £40m between them. None of them is still at the club. Each went back out in November as part of a cheaper deal for a younger, better player.

The accountants are not delighted.

The supporters, for once, are on their side.

* * *

# The Clear-Out

Having sealed relegation at relegation rivals Aston Villa, it was time to refresh the team for Division 2.

I don't want to put all the blame on my ageing centre-backs, but the time had come to move them on. This season I had three 90-rated CBs, all in their mid-30s. Meanwhile young Joane Gadou is knocking on the first-team door, Alessandro Marcandalli is developing nicely, Joe Rodon looks underrated, and Lucas Herrington and Jahmai Simpson-Pusey are pushing on too.

**Iñigo Martínez** (35, 90) went to Celtic, in return for young Ecuador midfielder Denil Castillo.

**Stefan de Vrij** (34, 90) brought a massive cash offer. The cash would have helped build more seats in the stadium and improve the training facilities, but I'd always rather take a punt on a player. So he went to Stuttgart for £10m and 20-year-old winger Jones El-Abdellaoui.

Thanks to Hugo and Rob for those deals.

**Juan Jesus** (35, 88) had a remarkable year, rising from 86 to 88 in his mid-30s. He went to Rubin Kazan for £15.1m.

The biggest decision was **Kevin De Bruyne**.

At 35 and rated 93, he was still our best player. He was also our most valuable one. Bayern offered **Mikel Jauregizar**, a 22-year-old La Liga starter rated 89, in a straight swap.

I'd said in advance that I needed at least an 88 back for KDB. I got an 89, thirteen years younger.

The club shop has, once again, a warehouse full of unsold "Kevin" shirts.

# The Verdict

Season 28 was a bad season made worse by bad decisions, and most of those decisions were mine.

But Hamburg have been here before. We won Division 2 in Season 25. We go into Season 29 with a younger squad, one of the strongest XIs in the division, and a manager who has finally learned to leave the tactics alone.

Probably.

Auf Wiedersehen, Division 1.

See you soon.
