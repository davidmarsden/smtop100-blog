# Your vote actually belongs to you now

Top 100 has always had polls, votes and manager opinions.

What has changed is the bit underneath them.

Community Polls now use verified manager accounts instead of relying on anonymous forms and typed names.

The public home for Community Polls and results is:

[https://vote.smtop100.blog/](https://vote.smtop100.blog/)

## Why change the old way?

Because anonymous forms are easy to set up and surprisingly awkward to trust.

If a vote is meant to be **one manager, one vote**, then the system needs to know who is actually voting.

Typing a name into a form is not much of an identity system. It creates obvious problems with duplicate votes, spelling variations, managers who have left, and arguments about who was eligible when voting opened.

The new system fixes that at the source.

## A verified electorate

When an eligible Top 100 manager signs in, the voting system uses the same approved identity as the Manager Portal.

That means the ballot belongs to the manager account rather than to whatever name happened to be typed into a box.

The electorate for a vote can also be recorded when the poll opens, so the rules do not quietly change halfway through because a manager joins or leaves.

That makes the result more auditable without making voting harder for managers.

## You can edit your ballot

A proper account also means a vote does not have to be treated as a one-shot anonymous submission.

Where the poll allows it, you can return before the deadline and update your ballot.

The system still knows it is your one vote. You are simply changing that vote rather than submitting another one.

## Public results stay public

You do not need a manager account just to see published Community Poll results.

That is an important principle across the rebuilt Top 100 ecosystem: authentication should add the actions that belong to a manager, not hide public information unnecessarily.

Voting is a manager action. Published results are part of the public record.

## Community Polls are not Manager Awards

The two systems share some infrastructure, but they serve different purposes.

**Community Polls** are general Top 100 voting and consultation.

**Manager Awards** are the end-of-season Awards process, with their own categories, history and Hall of Fame.

Keeping those two things visibly separate makes both clearer.

## What about Top 100 Regen?

The shared manager identity already understands Top 100 Regen as a separate game world, but Community Poll voting is currently Top 100-focused.

The architecture has been built so separate Regen polls can be added later without mixing the two electorates.

That matters. Shared infrastructure should not mean shared voting rolls.

## Where to go

For Community Polls and published results:

**[Community Polls](https://vote.smtop100.blog/)**

When a manager vote is open, sign in with the same account you use for the Manager Portal.

One manager. One account. One vote.

It is a small sentence, but it has taken quite a lot of plumbing to make it actually true.
