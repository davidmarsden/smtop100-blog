# Do managers who move clubs leave them worse off? What 28 seasons say

*A follow-up to [Apply to manage a vacant club](https://smtop100.blog/2026/10/29/apply-to-manage-a-vacant/).*

Every vacancy round brings the same worry: managers hop from club to club, win a promotion, move on to a bigger job and leave a wreck behind them. Should moving count against you? Should loyalty count for more?

It's a fair question, so we checked. The AI has crunched every final league table in Top 100's records, Seasons 1 to 28: **540 spells**, each one a manager's unbroken run at one club. Then we compared Top 100 with real football.

## How we measured it

Each club's final position is turned into an overall rank from 1 to 100. Division 1 champions are 1st, and the bottom club in Division 5 is 100th, so lower is better. For each spell, we compared three ranks:

- the club's rank the season **before** the manager arrived: what they inherited;
- its rank in the manager's **last** season;
- its rank the season **after** they left.

## Managers who move clubs leave them better, not worse

| How the spell ended | Spells | Rank inherited | Rank in last season | Rank the season after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moved to another club, after managing 5+ clubs | 161 | 53.0 | **50.4** | 51.3 |
| Moved to another club, after 1–4 clubs | 103 | 56.1 | **54.7** | 54.8 |
| Left Top 100, after 1–4 clubs | 70 | 54.2 | **48.7** | 50.3 |
| Left Top 100, after 5+ clubs | 54 | 41.7 | **44.8** | 48.3 |

Even the managers who move most often left their clubs higher than they found them, by nearly three places on average. Their clubs held most of that gain the following season.

There is one group who leave clubs worse off: managers who've had five or more clubs and then **leave Top 100 altogether**. Their clubs dropped three places on their watch and kept sliding after they'd gone. The problem isn't managers who move. It's managers who drift away, and that's what the activity, Gold and login rules are for.

## The one real risk: moving straight after a promotion

| After winning promotion, the manager… | Promotions | Relegated the next season |
|---|---|---|
| Stayed | 301 | **14%** |
| Moved to another club | 73 | **32%** |
| Left Top 100 | 28 | **32%** |

A promoted club is more than twice as likely to go straight back down if its manager leaves. The rate is the same whether the manager moved to another club or quit the game. That suggests it's about losing the manager and their plan at a fragile moment, not about squads being stripped on the way out.

It's worth weighing when a manager applies to move after a promotion. But even 32% is well below real football.

## And the squads they leave behind?

League positions are only half the argument. The other half is the squad: does a manager leave behind players the next manager can build on, or a team with no pulse?

Stats & History rates every club's squad strength each season, and that rating can be ranked across Top 100 from Season 6 to Season 27. So for every manager who moved club or left, we compared the squad's rank the season before they arrived with its rank in their last season.

| How the spell ended | Spells | Squad rank change | Squad improved | Squad got weaker |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moved to another club, after managing 5+ clubs | 93 | +2.0 places | 42% | 47% |
| Moved to another club, after 1–4 clubs | 75 | −1.0 | 52% | 40% |
| **Left Top 100, after 5+ clubs** | 47 | **−7.5** | 34% | **55%** |
| Left Top 100, after 1–4 clubs | 50 | +6.5 | 54% | 34% |

On average, managers who move clubs leave the squad about where they found it. The squads that really suffer are, again, the ones left by managers who've hopped between clubs and then quit the game.

**Promotion winners build squads.** This is the clearest finding of all:

| Managers who moved after a spell… | Spells | Squad rank change | Squad improved | Squad got weaker |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **With a promotion** | 64 | **+8.5 places** | **69%** | 30% |
| Without a promotion | 104 | −4.1 | 33% | 53% |

Managers who won promotion before moving on left their squads **more than eight places stronger** on average, and stronger in more than two cases out of three. The managers who left squads weaker were mostly the ones who moved *without* winning anything.

So a promotion isn't a low bar that lets managers strip a club and move on. It's the best sign we have that a manager builds squads as well as results. It also explains the relegation risk above: those clubs don't go back down because the squad has been stripped. They lose the manager.

**Time helps too.** Managers who stayed six seasons or more before moving left squads nearly four places stronger, more than any other group of movers. Building a squad over several seasons works, and the appointment score already rewards it.

## How Top 100 compares with real football

**Promoted clubs.** In the Premier League, a little under half of promoted clubs go straight back down: about **44–47%** since 1992 ([The Analyst](https://theanalyst.com/2025/04/premier-league-promoted-sides-relegation-championship); [NBC Sports](https://nbcsports.com/soccer/news/the-history-of-promotion-and-relegation-in-the-premier-league-era-pro-rel)). All three promoted clubs went down in both 2023/24 and 2024/25 ([Tales from the Top Flight](https://www.talesfromthetopflight.com/2025/05/16/how-many-times-have-all-promoted-teams-been-relegated-straight-back-down/)). In Top 100 the figure is about **19%**, and still only 32% when the manager leaves.

**Time in the job.** Real managers get very little time. The managers sacked in the Premier League in 2025/26 had lasted under a season on average, the shortest the League Managers Association has recorded ([Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/13574814/premier-league-managers-2026-27-record-new-bosses-manager-changes-and-tenure)). Across English football, managers' average time in post has long been under two years ([Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11688/9875915/average-tenure-of-managers-in-england-just-1-23-years)), and less in the lower leagues. The top level is the least patient of all.

Top 100 is the other way round:

| Starting division | Average spell | Lasted 5+ seasons |
|---|---|---|
| Division 1 | **4.2 seasons** | 36% |
| Division 2 | 3.2 seasons | 22% |
| Division 3 | 2.9 seasons | 15% |
| Division 4 | 3.3 seasons | 21% |
| Division 5 | 3.6 seasons | 24% |

Top 100's Division 1 managers get the most time of anyone, in a game world where every season is a full 21 weeks.

**Does changing the manager help?** Research on real football says a new manager rarely makes much difference on average. The famous "new manager bounce" is mostly regression to the mean: teams on a bad run tend to recover whether or not the manager changes. Studies of the Dutch top flight and of Europe's top five leagues reach the same conclusion ([Erasmus University](https://www.eur.nl/en/news/sacking-football-managers-does-not-improve-performance-study-finds); [van Ours and van Tuijl, 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10645-016-9277-0); [Erasmus University, 2026](https://www.eur.nl/en/ese/news/irrational-managerial-changes-remain-norm-europes-top-football-leagues)). It's one reason Top 100 gives bottom-three managers the benefit of the doubt.

## What it means for appointments

- **Winning should open doors.** Managers who earn promotions and titles leave their clubs higher, and their squads stronger, than they found them. They're exactly who should get the bigger jobs.
- **Loyalty already counts.** Stability is worth up to 20 points in the appointment score, including credit for seasons at your current club. Squad building counts too, through places climbed in the squad-strength ranking during your current spell.
- **Some appointments won't work out.** That's true in real football as well. The alternative, a game world where nobody ever gets the chance to step up, would be worse for everyone.
- **We'll keep an eye on moves straight after a promotion.** That's where the evidence shows a real cost.
