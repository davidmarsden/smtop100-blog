# Tournaments: Youth Cup, World Club Cup and the new Tournament Manager

Some parts of Top 100 are best told as stories.

Tournaments are not one of them.

When you want to know who you are playing, what the table looks like, whether a result has been entered, or who is waiting in the next knockout round, you need structure.

That is why tournaments now have a dedicated home:

[https://tournaments.smtop100.blog/](https://tournaments.smtop100.blog/)

## Youth Cup and World Club Cup are front and centre

The Tournament Manager is not just a generic database with a mysterious **Tournaments** label on the door.

The Youth Cup and World Club Cup are important parts of Top 100 in their own right, so the rebuilt navigation names them clearly and takes you straight to the competition you actually care about.

Public fixtures, groups, tables, knockout rounds and results remain public. You do not need a manager account just to follow the competition.

## What the Tournament Manager does

Behind the public pages is a much more structured competition system than we had before.

It can handle group stages, knockout rounds, one-leg and two-leg ties, byes, forfeits, double forfeits, fictional extra time and the practical messiness that appears once a tournament meets real managers.

The aim is not to automate every decision. It is to make the routine stuff reliable so organisers can spend their time on the decisions that actually need a human.

## Managers get their own view

If you have a verified manager account, the Tournament Manager can recognise the club attached to your identity.

That means your Manager Portal can show your own competition fixtures instead of making you search through the entire tournament.

For competitions using the shared system, managers can also submit their own results and forfeits, subject to the safeguards built into the competition workflow.

That is a fairly big shift from the old model where an organiser often had to enter everything manually.

## Registration is moving into the same system

The same account identity can also be used when tournament registration is open.

Instead of entering the same manager and club information over and over again, the Tournament Manager can work from the approved manager identity already attached to your account.

It is a small thing, but those small things are exactly what make the whole ecosystem less fiddly.

## Top 100 Regen uses it too

The Tournament Manager is shared infrastructure rather than something hard-coded only for Top 100.

Top 100 Regen remains a separate game world with its own managers, clubs and seasons, but it can use the same tournament machinery.

That is already happening with Pane Trifunov's **Season 4 Youth Cup**.

So the shared system gives us the best of both worlds: one well-tested tournament engine, without pretending Top 100 and Top 100 Regen are the same competition.

## What stays human

A more capable Tournament Manager does not mean every judgement call gets handed to software.

Organisers still matter. Disputes still need context. Exceptional cases still need people.

The point is to remove avoidable admin: calculating tables, tracking progression, preserving result history and giving managers a direct route to the tasks that belong to them.
