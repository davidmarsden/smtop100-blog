# One Top 100, many doors: how the new site fits together

Top 100 has changed quite a bit recently.

The main blog has moved, Top 100 Regen now lives alongside it, the old archives have been separated from the live site, tournaments have their own home, voting has been rebuilt, Manager Awards have been cleaned up, and managers now have proper accounts.

That is all good news — but only if people can work out where everything is.

So this is the first in a short **New Top 100** series explaining what each part of the rebuilt ecosystem does, why it exists, and where to find it.

## The main site is still home

The main address remains:

[https://smtop100.blog/](https://smtop100.blog/)

That is where the public life of Top 100 happens: news, articles, season pieces, team writing, announcements and the long-running archive of posts stretching back more than a decade.

The move to Micro.blog did not mean throwing all the specialist tools into one giant website. In fact, we have done almost the opposite.

The main site is the front door. The specialist parts of Top 100 each get a home of their own.

## Explore is the map

If you are not sure where something lives, start here:

[https://smtop100.blog/explore/](https://smtop100.blog/explore/)

Explore links together the parts of the ecosystem that managers and readers use most: Youth Cup, World Club Cup, Stats & History, Community Polls, Manager Awards, Search, Categories, Write, Subscribe and Support.

Think of it as the map rather than another destination you need to remember.

## Tournaments have their own centre

The Tournament Manager lives at:

[https://tournaments.smtop100.blog/](https://tournaments.smtop100.blog/)

The **Youth Cup** is now run there, with structured fixtures, groups, tables, knockout rounds, registrations, results and the Tournament Manager tools used by organisers and managers.

The public Youth Cup page now puts the **Schedule** first, with a clear route into **My Matches** when you want the personalised answer to a simpler question: what do I need to do next?

The **World Club Cup** is still on its current main-site archive route for now and will move into Tournament Manager next season. Explore names both competitions clearly so you can get to the right current destination without having to understand the plumbing underneath.

Public tournament information stays public. Signing in adds personal tools such as your own fixtures, result submission and other manager actions where the competition is using the shared Tournament Manager.

## Stats & History is not the blog archive

The history site lives at:

[https://archive.smtop100.blog/](https://archive.smtop100.blog/)

Its job is structured Top 100 history: seasons, managers, clubs, records and long-term statistics.

That is different from the chronological archive of old blog posts. One tells you what was written; the other helps tell you what happened.

## Voting has a proper home

Community Polls and their public results live at:

[https://vote.smtop100.blog/](https://vote.smtop100.blog/)

Manager Awards live separately at:

[https://awards.smtop100.blog/](https://awards.smtop100.blog/)

That distinction matters. Community Polls are general Top 100 democracy. Manager Awards are the end-of-season Awards system and its historical record.

They now share a proper authenticated manager identity underneath, but they are not the same thing.

## Managers now have My Matches

The manager account area is here:

[https://manager.smtop100.blog/](https://manager.smtop100.blog/)

The public-facing manager tool is now called **My Matches**. It uses your verified manager identity to show the fixtures and actions that belong to you, rather than making you hunt through the whole tournament.

For Top 100 managers that currently includes Youth Cup reminders, voting, a personal fixture view, tournament progress and result submission. In the tournament action view, **YOU SEND** means you need to send the Soccer Manager friendly request; **THEY SEND** means you are waiting for the opponent and should chase if it has not arrived.

Top 100 Regen managers use the same account system too, although the current Regen use is narrower and is centred on the Tournament Manager and Pane Trifunov's Season 4 Youth Cup.

The important bit is that we are no longer building a different identity system every time we add a feature.

## Writing has moved too

Managers and contributors no longer need access to the old WordPress dashboard just to write something for Top 100.

The Publishing Desk lives at:

[https://write.smtop100.blog/](https://write.smtop100.blog/)

It gives us a cleaner route for contributed writing while preserving proper author credit on the main site.

## And Top 100 Regen?

Top 100 Regen has not been swallowed by Top 100.

It remains its own game world, with its own clubs, managers, seasons and competitions. Its public editorial home is now inside the main site at:

[https://smtop100.blog/regen/](https://smtop100.blog/regen/)

The two worlds can share useful infrastructure without pretending they are the same competition.

## Why build it this way?

Because Top 100 is now too big to be one blog with a few extra pages bolted on.

A post archive, a tournament engine, a voting system, a manager account system and a historical statistics database are different jobs. Giving each one a clear home makes them easier to improve without turning the main site into a maze.

The trick is making them feel like one family. That is why the sites now share clearer navigation and why Explore exists as the common map.

Over the next few posts in this series, we will take each part in turn and explain what has changed.

For now, if you remember only two addresses, make them these:

**Home:** [smtop100.blog](https://smtop100.blog/)

**Map:** [smtop100.blog/explore/](https://smtop100.blog/explore/)

Welcome to the new Top 100. Same world. Better plumbing.
