# Top 100 Regen has moved in — without becoming Top 100

Top 100 Regen now lives inside the main Top 100 website family.

That does **not** mean it has stopped being its own game world.

Its public home is now:

[https://smtop100.blog/regen/](https://smtop100.blog/regen/)

The old standalone domain redirects there, and the new section sits alongside the rest of the rebuilt ecosystem.

## Why bring Regen into the main site?

Because keeping two completely separate websites created duplicated work for very little benefit.

The two worlds already share history, people, ideas and infrastructure. It makes sense for them to share things like navigation, publishing, manager identity and tournament tools where that helps.

What did not make sense was maintaining a second mini-ecosystem just to prove they were different.

## What remains separate?

Almost everything that matters competitively.

Top 100 Regen still has its own:

- managers;
- clubs;
- seasons;
- competition history;
- tournament entries;
- rules and context.

A Top 100 manager is not automatically a Regen manager, and a Regen manager is not automatically part of the Top 100 voting electorate.

The worlds can share machinery without sharing data that should remain distinct.

## One account system, two game worlds

The Manager Portal already understands both Top 100 and Top 100 Regen.

When claiming an account, a manager chooses the correct game world and club. The identity is then checked within that world.

That means we do not need a second authentication system just for Regen.

At the moment, Regen's use of manager accounts is deliberately narrower than Top 100's.

The main live use is the **Tournament Manager**, including Pane Trifunov's **Season 4 Youth Cup**.

That is enough to prove the model: Regen can use shared tournament and account infrastructure without inheriting every Top 100 feature by accident.

## Pane's Season 4 Youth Cup

The current Regen Youth Cup is a good example of the new setup.

It can use the same Tournament Manager machinery for fixtures, progression and manager actions, while remaining clearly a Top 100 Regen competition.

That saves us from building and maintaining a separate tournament engine for the second world.

## What about polls and Awards?

Those are not shared across worlds today.

Top 100 Community Polls and Manager Awards are currently scoped to Top 100 managers.

The underlying manager identity has been designed so Regen-specific voting and Awards can be added later without mixing electorates or records.

That is the pattern we want to keep using: shared foundations, explicit world boundaries.

## Why the name matters

Publicly, it is **Top 100 Regen**.

Not a generic “Regen” tab. Not a side project hidden under Tournaments. It is a distinct game world with its own identity and history.

Bringing it under the main site should make it easier to find, not make it disappear into Top 100.
