# Manager Awards: voting, history and the Hall of Fame

Manager Awards have always been one of the more enjoyable bits of Top 100.

They are also one of the bits most worth preserving properly.

The rebuilt Manager Awards site now does two jobs at once: it handles current Awards activity and it protects the historical record of previous winners.

You can find it here:

[https://awards.smtop100.blog/](https://awards.smtop100.blog/)

## Awards are not just another poll

It would have been easy to fold Manager Awards into the general Community Polls site and call the job done.

We deliberately did not do that.

Community Polls are for general Top 100 voting and consultation.

Manager Awards are a specific end-of-season tradition with their own categories, voting process, records and history.

The underlying voting machinery can be shared without flattening the two things into one.

## Voting now uses manager accounts

The biggest change is underneath the ballot.

Manager Awards voting now uses the same verified manager identity as the Manager Portal and Community Polls.

That means Awards no longer need to rely on typed names as proof of who is voting.

The same benefits apply here as elsewhere: one manager, one ballot; a clearly defined electorate; and a system that can preserve who was eligible when the Awards round opened.

## The Hall of Fame matters

The Manager Awards site is not only about the current season.

The historical Hall of Fame and Awards record are a big part of why the site exists at all.

Previous winners should not disappear just because we rebuilt the voting system.

So the new site separates current Awards activity from the historical side clearly enough that you can use it for either purpose.

If you want to vote, there is a current Awards route.

If you want to look back at previous winners and records, the history is there too.

## Why keep the history separate from the blog?

Because Awards history is structured information.

A blog post announcing a winner is useful and worth preserving. But if you want to know who has won most often, who won a particular division in a particular season, or how the Hall of Fame has changed over time, you should not have to search through years of announcement posts.

The Awards site turns that history into something you can actually browse.

## What stays public?

The historical Awards record and published results are public.

Signing in is only needed for the manager action: voting.

That is the same principle used elsewhere in the new ecosystem. Public record stays public; manager identity is used where identity genuinely matters.

## What about Top 100 Regen?

Top 100 Regen Awards are not yet running through the shared Awards system.

The manager identity underneath is already world-aware, so a Regen-specific Awards system can be added later without mixing Regen managers into the Top 100 electorate or history.

Separate game world. Shared foundations.
